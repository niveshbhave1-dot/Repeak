# Repeak — product design handoff

## Point of view
Repeak is the meeting point between a training log and a trailhead. The feed leads with a reason to participate, then shows verified effort from real-feeling peers. The primary challenge action is visible without requiring a detail view. The organizer experience carries the same challenge component into a light, information-dense operational context.

## Prototype scope
This is an interactive, responsive front-end concept. Activity, athletes, dates, and organizer statistics are representative sample content; actions are session-only and do not connect to real accounts or activity tracking. No Figma file or shareable Figma link is generated by the web implementation.

## Foundations
- Grid: 4px base; the core rhythm is 8/16/24/32/40/48px.
- Colors: semantic light/dark variables in `src/styles.css`. Primary ink is deep evergreen, signal is coral, elevation/progress accent is spring green, surfaces are warm off-white. The organizer uses the same roles in a deliberately lighter operational layout.
- Type: Archivo for display, numbers, and identity; DM Sans for interface copy; selective Georgia italic for expressive phrases.
- Corner radius: 4px for surfaces; 2px for action controls. Avatars and dots are circular.
- Breakpoints: three-column layout above 1080px, two-column 701–1080px, single-column below 701px. Bottom navigation anchors the mobile experience. Extra-small 320–375px adjustments protect text and metric layout.
- Imagery: three locally bundled, original generated editorial photographs. All visible subjects and activity categories are endurance-related.

## Reusable parts and exports
- `src/components/repeak/brand.tsx`: editable inline SVG `PeakMark`, `RepeakCoin`, `TrailIcon`, `CompassIcon`, and `SummitIcon`. Copy the `<svg>` markup from each component into any vector editor; its geometry is path-based and resolution-independent.
- `src/routes/index.tsx`: `ChallengeCard`, `ActivityCard`, `RideCard`, `ProgressPanel`, `Avatar`, `SectionHeader`, navigation, and organizer adaptation. Shared challenge copy and image intentionally bridge consumer and B2B views.
- `public/favicon.svg`: standalone vector application mark.
- `src/styles.css`: semantic tokens, typography, grids, components, and responsive measurements.

## Behavior and states
- Feed tabs switch among personalized, followed, and challenge-focused content.
- Challenge photo opens details. Joining updates the action, progress panel, coin balance, profile, and organizer participant total. Challenge join remains a local prototype state.
- Activity kudos and saves toggle; comments open a composer and append on submit. Share uses native share when available, otherwise copies the page URL.
- Discover searches sample adventures and filters Challenges/Events. Event participation toggles its action.
- Rewards shows the original coin and milestones. Profile links to discovery and organizer mode.
- Organizer Overview presents participation KPIs and the challenge adapted as an operational summary; Participants switches to a roster. Athlete view returns to the consumer feed.
- Notifications open a lightweight popover. Buttons have focus-visible indicators. Reduced-motion preferences disable transitions.

## Design trade-offs
- The challenge is above community activity to answer “what can I do?” before “what happened?” This is a deliberate participation-first prioritization.
- Athlete performance is compact and verified-looking to create credibility without turning the feed into a spreadsheet.
- Organizer adaptation is visibly connected to the consumer challenge, but removes the immersive overlay in favor of readable metrics and an at-a-glance status.
- The experience avoids fabricated backend verification: the verified label is sample UI, not a claim that this prototype validates GPS workouts.
